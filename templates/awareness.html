Disability Awareness

Driving community inclusion starts with understanding. Explore the categories below to learn basic disability etiquette and how you can foster a more inclusive environment.

Physical Disabilities

Includes mobility impairments that may require wheelchairs, walkers, or canes.

Etiquette: Speak directly to the person, not their companion. Do not lean on or move a person's wheelchair without permission, as it is part of their personal space.

Visual Disabilities

Includes partial vision loss to complete blindness.

Etiquette: Always introduce yourself when approaching. Offer your arm for guidance rather than grabbing theirs. Describe the setting or obstacles clearly.

Hearing Disabilities

Includes varying degrees of hearing loss or deafness.

Etiquette: Ensure you have their attention before speaking. Speak clearly without shouting, and keep your face visible for lip-reading. Use gestures or write if necessary.

Intellectual Disabilities

Includes conditions affecting cognitive functioning and life skills.

Etiquette: Treat adults as adults. Use plain language and give the person time to process information and respond. Be patient and listen actively.
